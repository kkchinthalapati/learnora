Calm by default, honest by construction

An evolution of the current paper-and-teal language rather than a replacement. Students already chose it and it already tests AA. What changes: fewer fonts, no glass, no spring easing, tighter colour roles, and components for the parts that make Learnora different: mix-ups, hints, uncertainty and waiting.

01 · Colour

Every colour has a job, and the job is in its name

Each role has four tokens. fill is for shapes and must be ≥3:1 against the surface. text is for words and must be ≥4.5:1 on bg, surface and soft. soft is a tinted background. on-fill is for words placed on the fill. Red appears only for genuine errors and destructive actions. "Untested" and "behind" never get a semantic colour.

NeutralWarm, chroma under 0.02. Carries 90% of every screen.

02 · Type

Serif for the app's voice, sans for everything you read

Why I kept Newsreader

It gives Learnora a calm, bookish voice that sets it apart from loud study apps, and it marks the difference between the app speaking ("Welcome back") and the content you're studying. Liam's problem was serif body text, not headings. So the rules are: only Display, H1 and H2, never below 22 px, never in a question, answer or note, and reading mode swaps it for sans.

Three families, down from four

Newsreader (display) · Instrument Sans (body, UI and tabular numbers) · Atkinson Hyperlegible (reading mode only, loaded only when it's on). JetBrains Mono stays, but only on code blocks. That's one less font file for Aarav to download.

Osmosis

Osmosis is when water moves across a membrane. It goes from where there is more water to where there is less.

The membrane lets water through, but not bigger things like sugar. That is why a raisin swells up in water.

  • Body text goes from 16 to 19 px and line height from 1.55 to 1.75, with a little extra letter and word spacing.
  • Atkinson Hyperlegible, and headings switch to sans. Always left-aligned, never justified, no italics.
  • Line length capped at 60ch. Paragraphs get space between them instead of first-line indents.
  • Every long text gets a read-aloud button. Reading mode is offered the first time a passage runs past 80 words.
