Learnora · design direction · Sept 2026

"I can figure this out."

Learnora already has strong parts: a forecast engine, a misconception ledger, spaced repetition, and five AI study tools. The problem is that they arrive as about twenty separate destinations. This direction regroups them into one idea: every visit is a session with a clear goal, and every session ends with evidence of learning.

Sources cited in the rationale

  • Bastani et al., "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning," PNAS, June 2025: practice with GPT-4 raised practice scores but reduced performance once the AI was removed. Teacher-designed hints in place of answers reduced the harm.
  • Rohrer, Dedrick, Hartwig & Cheung, 2020 (IES summary): interleaved practice, 61% vs 38% on an unannounced test, d = 0.83, across 54 seventh-grade classes. Notes on the paper record that teachers found interleaved sets took longer.
  • Trophy, "Apps that use streaks" (Mar 2026): for learners who are there because they have to be, heavy streaks can feel patronising. This is industry analysis, not peer-reviewed research.
  • AppStorys (Jul 2026): harsh streak resets drive abandonment, so recovery paths are recommended. Industry guidance.

What the sources support: guardrailed hints over answers, and mixing topics in practice. What I inferred: confidence ratings, the four-step mastery ladder, and the weekly study-day goal. These are design choices, not tested results. They should be validated with Learnora's own learning-event data.
